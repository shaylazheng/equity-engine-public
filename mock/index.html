Gate, recomputed for this selection

Residual t and retention are from the fitted run in docs/go_no_go.md. Deflated Sharpe is recomputed here against the number of signals you include: fewer trials means a lower bar. Excluding the momentum or value factor on the Risk factors tab swaps in the ex-own-factor numbers.

The DSR here is an approximation. It assumes normal residual spreads (no skew or excess kurtosis) and a common 640-month sample, and reproduces the documented values to within about 0.04. The number that counts comes from scripts/go_no_go.py. Changing the threshold after seeing this table is the thing the worklog warns against, so any threshold you settle on here should be written into the script before the next fitted run.

Fitted factor premia, 1972-01 to 2025-12

Cross-sectional model, exposures lagged one month, 648 monthly fits, median 3,708 names, mean R² 0.106. Industries are constrained to sum to zero rather than dropped.

What excluding a factor does

  • momentum or value: a signal that loads on that style is no longer regressed on itself. The gate table switches to the ex-own column: momentum retention 33% → 98%, sales-to-price 44% → 68%, earnings yield 66% → 57%.
  • market: the level of the market has nowhere to live in the fit, and a long-only book's dominant risk goes unmodelled. The optimizer will confidently concentrate on whatever the misfit calls safe.
  • beta, size, or an industry: no precomputed result. The whole model must be re-fitted with scripts/fit_risk_model.py and the gate re-run.
  • The structural point: a factor cannot be both risk and alpha. Taking momentum out of the risk model is not a free upgrade for the momentum signal. It is a claim that momentum is an anomaly rather than a compensated risk, and that claim is what would need defending.
